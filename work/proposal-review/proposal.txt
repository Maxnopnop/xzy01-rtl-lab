1 Introduction 
1.1 Background and Engineering Problem 
Digital circuit design translates behavioural requirements into register-transfer level (RTL) descriptions that can be 
simulated and synthesized into hardware. Even a short specification requires precise decisions about interfaces, 
operand widths, signed arithmetic, clocking, reset priority and cycle latency. Large language models (LLMs) offer a 
way to generate an initial Verilog implementation from natural language. RTLCoder demonstrates how domain-
specific data and training can improve this capability [1]. For students and engineers, such assistance could reduce 
repetitive coding and make alternative implementations easier to explore, provided that the generated circuit is 
evaluated against its intended behaviour. 
The engineering problem is that plausible code is not sufficient evidence of a correct or useful design. Generated RTL 
can compile but produce incorrect outputs, mishandle boundary cases or fail synthesis. A benchmark failure may 
also arise from an incomplete description or an inconsistent testbench, as investigated by RTL-BenchMT [2]. Beyond 
correctness, Dr. RTL shows that tool-guided RTL changes can be studied for timing and area improvement under 
functional equivalence constraints [3]. These works motivate a workflow in which generation, verification and 
analysis are connected, and in which claims are supported by recorded experiments rather than selected successful 
examples. 
This project will first establish that workflow and compare the quality of Verilog generated by different LLMs. It will 
then use the observed failures to select a focused research problem, such as specification handling, relevant 
knowledge retrieval or feedback-based repair. The intended contribution is a carefully evaluated improvement and 
an explanation of where it works, supported by a usable research prototype. The initial scope covers small 
combinational, single-clock sequential and control modules. More demanding designs and RTL optimization will be 
considered after the team has established reliable simulation and synthesis. Early exploration will therefore lead to a 
defined investigation rather than an open-ended collection of features. 
1.2 Objectives 
The project combines an exploratory opening phase with measurable engineering deliverables. By the end of 
October, pilot experiments and a supervisor discussion will establish the principal improvement direction, model 
access, evaluation scope and revised task ownership. The core commitment is to build and evaluate the workflow 
and one focused method; a specific accuracy gain will not be assumed before the baseline is measured. The study 
aims for research-quality evidence and a presentation-ready demonstration, with publication considered if the 
findings warrant it.

1.2.2 Scope and Expected Contribution 
The expected research contribution is a targeted improvement to LLM-assisted RTL generation, supported by a 
controlled comparison and an analysis of failure mechanisms. Structured specification prompting, retrieval-
augmented generation (RAG) and EDA feedback are candidate approaches. They will be explored sufficiently to 
identify a defensible question, but the project will not attempt to develop every approach to equal depth. Existing 
methods will form the starting point; any claim of novelty will identify the specific difference from the closest prior 
work. 
The supporting engineering contribution will be a reproducible pipeline and a small research interface that displays 
the specification, generated RTL, tool outcomes and repair history. Benchmark-related contributions may include 
audited task descriptions, additional boundary tests or a carefully reviewed robustness subset. Merely comparing 
model scores will establish a baseline, but will not by itself be presented as a new generation method. 
Full model training, a production software service, a complete processor and chip fabrication are outside the initial 
commitment. A digital-design or EDA question-answering feature remains optional. RTL optimization is a possible 
research extension: it will proceed only when an appropriate synthesis flow, constraints, reference design and 
correctness check are available. These boundaries preserve time for an in-depth investigation without preventing a 
stronger direction from emerging. 
1.2.3 Initial Planning Targets 
The initial planning scale is a 10-task development set and a separate 40-task final evaluation set, provisionally 
balanced between VerilogEval and RTLLM. At least two accessible LLMs and three samples per task are planned for 
the main model comparison. These are workload estimates to be reviewed by 31 October, not claims about a 
completed dataset. If compatibility or cost requires a change, the revised target, selection rule and reason will be 
recorded before final evaluation. A benchmark subset will always be identified as a subset. 
Project completion will be assessed through validated tool behaviour, a fixed evaluation protocol, completed 
comparisons, a tested prototype and reproducible records. The scientific aim is to improve useful generation quality 
at a stated resource budget. A negative result will require a credible investigation of its causes and limits; it will not 
be concealed by changing the test set or redefining success after seeing the results. 
1.3 Literature Review of Existing Solutions 
Domain-specific generation with RTLCoder 
RTLCoder develops a fully open-source approach to RTL generation through automated instruction-code data 
construction and a training method that uses code-quality feedback [1]. It also investigates efficient deployment of a 
relatively small model. Its central lesson for this project is that generation quality depends on the interaction 
between data, model behaviour and evaluation. The reported comparisons concern the models and benchmark 
settings of that study; they do not establish a ranking of current services. We will examine its data and evaluation 
assumptions and consider an available open model where resources permit. Reproducing its full training pipeline is 
not necessary for the proposed inference-time investigation. 
Benchmark maintenance with RTL-BenchMT 
RTL-BenchMT studies flaws in RTL generation benchmarks and sensitivity to rewritten descriptions [2]. Its agent-
assisted processes inspect failures, propose revisions and review descriptions, with human oversight of accepted 
changes. This is relevant because a low pass rate can reflect errors in generated code, ambiguity in a specification, or 
disagreement between a specification and a test. We will therefore inspect the benchmark and runner before 
attributing every failure to a model. Semantic-preserving rewrites may be used as a robustness experiment, but 
degraded performance on a rewrite alone will not be treated as proof of training-data memorization. Our 
contribution would need to go beyond simply repeating the paper’s maintenance procedure. 
Tool-guided optimization with Dr. RTL 

===== PDF PAGE 5 =====
 
 
3 
Dr. RTL investigates RTL timing optimization using detailed EDA feedback, functional equivalence checks and 
reusable optimization knowledge [3]. Its evaluation emphasizes realistic starting designs and a commercial synthesis 
workflow. This extends the research question from whether generated RTL is correct to whether a functionally 
preserved implementation can be improved. It also raises the entry requirements: an optimization experiment needs 
consistent constraints, meaningful reports and a trustworthy equivalence procedure. We will use these 
requirements to assess the feasibility of an optimization extension, rather than assume that shorter code or fewer 
generic cells necessarily implies a better physical design. 
How these works shape the proposed study 
Together, the three recommended papers provide complementary views of model capability, evaluation validity and 
tool-guided improvement. The project will connect these views through a common experimental workflow. Its initial 
emphasis is on generation quality and error analysis; the precise method contribution will be selected from observed 
problems and positioned against the closest prior solution. The literature review will continue as that direction 
becomes more specific. 
VerilogEval and RTLLM as evaluation foundations 
VerilogEval provides executable evaluation of generated Verilog [4]. Its subsequent specification-to-RTL formulation 
is more closely aligned with this project than code completion alone [5], [6]. The selected task form, release and 
evaluation scripts must therefore be recorded. RTLLM considers syntax, functionality and design quality [7], 
supporting the distinction between generating legal code, satisfying functional tests and obtaining useful synthesized 
hardware. Its repository reports expanded coverage in v2.0 and description/testbench corrections in v2.1 [8]. We 
will start from a documented current snapshot and will not mix scores obtained with incompatible releases. 
The benchmarks provide a practical basis for comparison, but neither removes the need to validate the local tool 
setup or inspect selected tasks. Public tasks may have been present in model training or other evaluation material. 
Results will therefore be described as benchmark performance, with selected supplementary cases used to probe 
specific weaknesses. Any portability changes needed for the ECE server will be checked against the reference RTL 
and kept separate from changes intended to improve task quality. Scores on revised cases will not silently replace 
scores on the original version. 
Prompting and retrieval as candidate interventions 
Prompting can make interface and temporal requirements explicit or guide a model through a structured design 
check. RAG supplies retrieved external material to condition generation; the original RAG work motivates this use of 
external knowledge [9], but does not establish its effectiveness for RTL. For this project, candidate sources include 
reviewed Verilog rules, general design patterns and tool documentation. The value of retrieval will depend on 
whether those sources address errors that occur in the pilot. Irrelevant passages, incorrect versions and examples 
too close to test answers can invalidate the intended comparison. A retrieval method will consequently be evaluated 
with recorded sources and a no-retrieval or matched-context control. 
Proposed research questions 
RQ1: How do selected LLMs differ in functional pass rate, compilation and synthesis acceptance, failure categories 
and resource use on the same documented RTL tasks? 
RQ2: Can an intervention motivated by the observed failures improve functional generation quality over a fair 
baseline, and which component accounts for the change? 
RQ3: How stable are the findings across circuit classes, repeated samples and selected specification variants, and 
what practical limits remain? 
Optional RQ4: Once correctness and tooling are established, can a bounded RTL rewriting process improve a defined 
synthesis metric while preserving the required behaviour?

2 Methodology 
2.1 Overview 
2.1.1 System Description 
The proposed system will combine a versioned task repository, a model adapter, configurable prompting and 
retrieval, an EDA runner, an independent scoring stage and an experiment store. Each task will supply the functional 
description, required module interface and relevant clock, reset and latency constraints. The model adapter will 
produce a candidate Verilog module and retain the exact prompt, raw response and access settings. A common 
extraction policy will process every model output without manually fixing its logic. This arrangement supports both a 
cross-model baseline and a later comparison of generation methods. 
Compilation and self-checking simulation will establish the first level of evidence. The EDA runner will then perform 
synthesis screening, and collect area or timing information when a suitable library and constraint set have been 
validated. ECE server resources will be the primary execution environment. Cadence Xcelium and Genus are 
candidate simulation and synthesis tools; their availability and supported versions will be checked during setup [10]. 
An open-source path using Icarus Verilog and Yosys can support learning and fallback experiments [11], [12]. 
Different tool flows will be reported separately and will not be treated as interchangeable measurements. 
For an investigated repair method, compiler messages or dedicated diagnostic tests may inform a limited number of 
revisions. Final scoring tests and their outcomes will remain outside the generation and repair loop. All candidates 
will be retained, including failures. A local demonstration interface will show the input, generated code, recorded 
checks and any repair history. The interface will expose the evidence needed to interpret an output, while the 
experiment store supports batch analysis and replay. This design prioritizes a reliable research workflow over a large 
application feature set.

Each benchmark task will use a common machine-readable format containing the task ID, task name, natural-
language specification, required module name, exact module interface, clock and reset conventions, reference 
testbench, expected output format, difficulty level and circuit type. Additional metadata will identify the reference 
implementation, testbench top module, result-parsing convention, original source task and repository revision. 
Testbench and reference-implementation fields will point to versioned files to avoid maintaining inconsistent copies. 
The specification will define relevant boundary behaviour, including arithmetic overflow, reset priority, enable 
conditions, output latency and simultaneous operations where applicable. The testbench must evaluate behaviour 
required by the specification rather than introduce unstated requirements. Generation inputs will remain separate 
from reference implementations, scoring tests and expected outputs. 
Before inclusion in the frozen suite, each task must pass an interface and specification consistency check. Its 
reference implementation must pass the selected evaluation flow, and at least one deliberately incorrect 
implementation targeting a relevant requirement must be rejected. This fault check provides evidence that the test 
detects a known error; it does not establish exhaustive coverage. Unresolved contradictions will be corrected with a 
recorded justification or excluded before final evaluation. 
The pilot will establish tool timeouts, output limits, supported HDL constructs and practical runtime. The initial 
design scope excludes multi-clock interactions and physical implementation. A synthesis run will record the top 
module, library, timing constraints and tool settings. If only generic synthesis is available, acceptance and generic cell 
statistics will be reported without claims about silicon area, sign-off timing or power. 
2.1.4 ECE Knowledge 
ELEC 2350 Introduction to Computer Organization and Design provides a foundation for arithmetic, datapaths and 
control. We will apply this knowledge to specify operand widths, signedness and cycle-level behaviour, and develop 
reference implementations that help identify data-representation and update-order errors in generated RTL.

ELEC 3310 Digital Fundamentals and System Design supports sequential logic, RTL modelling and simulation. We will 
apply its principles to state machines, reset behaviour and testbench construction. Correct assignment semantics, 
waveform inspection and boundary tests will help distinguish circuit errors from testbench races or sampling 
mistakes. 
ELEC 4320 FPGA-based Design: From Theory to Practice connects Verilog development with synthesis, 
implementation and simulation. We will apply this knowledge to assess RTL synthesizability, interpret tool reports

later demonstration if feasible. 
COMP 4611 Design and Analysis of Computer Architectures provides methods for evaluating architectural choices 
and performance trade-offs. We will use these methods to reason about latency, throughput and pipelining, 
interpret experimental results, and assess whether proposed RTL changes offer meaningful benefits under consistent 
design constraints.

2.2.3 Focused Method Development 
Objective 3: Develop and evaluate one focused improvement to RTL generation, using a baseline, a proposed 
method and at least one ablation or matched-budget control to determine its benefits and limitations. 
O3.1 Design the intervention and comparison  
WU, Yuzhen 
Turn the October findings into a specific hypothesis and mechanism. For example, if failures repeatedly involve reset 
priority or output latency, test whether a structured specification checklist reduces these errors. All conditions must 
receive the same design facts: adding missing requirements only to the proposed method would confound the 
comparison. Define the primary outcome, baseline, expected affected error classes and meaningful improvement 
criterion on development data before final testing. Position the selected intervention against the closest published 
approach and identify the actual proposed difference. 
O3.2 Implement targeted retrieval or feedback  
MA, Shenhan 
Implement the selected method as a configurable component. For retrieval, index reviewed language rules, generic 
design notes or versioned tool documentation, preserving source identifiers. Form queries from the specification or 
permitted diagnostics; tune passage selection on development tasks. Exclude exact and near-duplicate task 
solutions. Compare with the same prompt without retrieval, and use a fixed-context control if the claim concerns 
retrieval selection rather than simply additional information. Record retrieved passages to explain both 
improvements and regressions. 
For repair, supply the original requirements, current RTL and a bounded compiler or diagnostic-test summary. Start 
with a maximum of two repairs per run and a fixed stopping policy. Diagnostic tests may guide repairs; final scoring 
tests may not. Preserve every candidate and score the terminal output without choosing the best one using hidden 
results. If the claim concerns the benefit of feedback, include fresh generation with an equivalent call budget. 
Retrieval and repair will not both become mandatory full studies unless the chosen question requires their 
interaction. 
WU, Yuzhen will lead implementation of the selected generation intervention. MA, Shenhan will provide permitted 
compiler or diagnostic feedback if repair is included, while LI, Aojie will maintain the task definitions and separation 
between development material and final evaluation resources.

synthesizable. 
Repair outcome Terminal-candidate functional pass rate under the fixed repair limit; fraction of initially failing 
runs repaired; regressions and calls used. Keep separate from first-generation scores. 
Resources Wall-clock time, model calls, reported tokens and measured expenditure. Report unavailable 
billing data as unavailable; do not equate provider token counts with identical compute.  
Design quality extension Mapped area and timing under identical libraries and constraints, for designs passing the stated 
correctness checks. Generic cell counts are reported only as structural statistics.  
 
Model comparison and method comparison answer different questions. The former changes the model while 
holding the task protocol fixed; the latter fixes the model while changing the intervention. Model selection and 
prompt tuning use development data. A baseline may be reused only when task, model version, settings and 
evaluation conditions match the method experiment. Otherwise it must be rerun. Defaults that differ across services 
will be documented rather than silently described as identical. 

===== PDF PAGE 13 =====
 
 
11 
For N tasks and R samples per task, the sample-averaged first-generation pass rate is the number of passing first 
candidates divided by N × R. Report this as a pass@1 estimate under the stated sampling settings, not as pass@R. 
The planning scenario of two models, 40 evaluation tasks and three samples gives 240 initial generations. Two 
additional method conditions on one model add 240 generations. A repair study on one 120-run condition permits at 
most 240 additional repair calls; a matched-budget regeneration control, pilot and reruns require separate 
allowance. 
Uncertainty will be estimated by resampling whole tasks with their repeated samples grouped together. Paired 
differences will use the same resampled tasks across methods, with benchmark membership preserved. Report 
separate benchmark scores and a clearly labelled pooled subset score. Missing, malformed and timed-out outputs 
count as failures in the primary end-to-end measure. Infrastructure failures will also be reported separately, with a 
bounded technical retry policy fixed in advance. No favourable sample or task will be selected retrospectively.
